# 7. Designed for 10–100× the current corpus

Part of [Design constraints](../constraints.md).

The corpus was measured in the millions of records and several GiB of bronze mid-backfill, having more than
doubled within hours. Assume a user with 100× that. A
design that works only at today's size is not finished — and "works" includes completing at all, since a
whole-corpus reader has been found dying with a V8 out-of-memory fatal at Node's default heap ceiling.

**Memory is the axis that arrives silently.** Nothing warned as the corpus grew past the ceiling; the verb the
product is named for simply stopped working one day, with a 46-frame native stack trace in place of an error.
Three rules follow, and all three are mechanical rather than advisory:

- **A load that would not fit REFUSES, typed, before V8 kills the process.** The bronze reader checks the
  real heap between read chunks (`src/lib/heapBudget.ts`, `src/corpus/corpusDirRead.ts`). The refusal names the corpus, the ceiling, the flag that
  raises it and the fact that the flag is a mask.

  **A safety check that allocates has a failure mode its own subject was supposed to prevent**, so it decides
  whether it can afford to run before it runs. Measured: the whole-corpus reader at the default
  ceiling died with `FATAL ERROR: Ineffective mark-compacts near heap limit`, native frame 10
  `v8::internal::GCExtension::GC` — INSIDE the forced collection the guard performs on its own failing path.
  `guardHeap` collects and re-asks because `used_heap_size` counts garbage, but within a hair of the raw
  ceiling the collection is itself the last allocation. A raised `--max-old-space-size` is never the fix — the
  owner's ruling — because the corpus keeps growing and the next ceiling arrives the same way.

  **The collector's reserve is not the load's.** A collection check sharing the LOAD's 15% reserve refuses to
  collect from 85% of the ceiling upwards — the exact band where the heap is fullest of garbage — so every
  refusal past it quotes a garbage-inclusive `used_heap_size`. The bands are separate (`HEAP_RESERVE_FRACTION`
  15% for the load, `COLLECT_RESERVE_FRACTION` 5% for the collector, `src/lib/heapBudget.ts`), bracketed by
  the reading that must stay refused (4,215 MiB live of 4,288) and the one that must be collectable (5,823 of
  6,848, after reading 1,323 MiB of disk). Measured: `derive` at `--max-old-space-size=6656` read
  1,323 MiB with a shared band and **4,219 MiB with separate ones**.

- **A reader that cannot fit the corpus should carry less of it, not be given a bigger heap.** Measured:
  `derive` reading the whole `bronze` projection refused on an 8 GiB corpus needing about twice that
  against a 4,288 MiB ceiling — on EVERY `refresh`, since refresh derives by default. Over a sample of tens of thousands of records
  across all six sources, `text` is about two fifths of bronze's bytes and `raw` is a little over a third, and silver reads neither
  wholesale — exactly one detector reads `text` (a regex the projection precomputes into
  `CorpusRecord.unresolved`) and four `raw` scalars are read in total. So `derive` is its own
  `CorpusProjection` beside `bronze` and `retrieval`, and runs that corpus in 46 seconds. Its band was measured per source with
  `slopweaver dev heap-curve --projection derive --corpus ~/.slopweaver/corpus/bronze/<source>` and is
  0.05…0.70 live bytes per disk byte, against `bronze`'s 0.7…2.0. The owner's ruling is the general form: an
  operation needing 20, 8 or even 4 GB is rejected and fixed properly, never taped over.

- **A GATE that outgrows this machine moves to CI; the ceiling still does not move.** The two bullets above
  are about readers, and the owner extends the same rule to the development gates: raising the heap ceiling
  without reducing what the underlying code needs is felt more over time, until the number of concurrent slots
  has to drop to avoid kernel panics. The owner's fix is a different machine rather than a bigger number: checks
  that would run on this machine run on CI instead, and the owner and the fleet watch CI. So the
  `pre-push` hook's `yarn validate:local` is an opt-in lane: it runs only the gates CI cannot
  see the subject of, and every other gate, typecheck and both test lanes run only on the runner — which
  runs all of it anyway, so the offload adds no CI time
  ([toolchain/rebaseline-and-lanes.md](../toolchain/rebaseline-and-lanes.md#two-lanes-the-local-one-is-a-secrets-scan-and-a-working-tree-check-and-everything-else-is-cis) § Two lanes). What this does NOT license: shrinking a gate's verdict to fit,
  or raising `HEAP_BUDGET_MIB` to buy a green. That budget is a tripwire a session never raises: over it,
  cut what the gate holds, and a requirement still past it goes to the owner (`src/devLint/namedObjectParamsHeap.ts`).
  **A gate holds this rather than prose**: `heap-ceiling-ratchet` pins every heap number the repository
  spends — each package script's `--max-old-space-size` or compiler owner's `--rss-ceiling-mib`, each lint
  gate's argv ceiling, `HEAP_BUDGET_MIB` —
  in `.baselines/heap-ceilings.json`, and against the base commit CI names refuses any raise, or a new
  ceiling over 4,096 MiB, without an appended raise record anchoring the owner's word. Lowering needs only the pin.

- **A guard's ADVICE must be reachable in ONE step, or it is not advice.** A refusal that names a ceiling is
  telling a caller what to do next, and that number is answering a different question from the one the
  refusal answers. Measured on a corpus of about 8 GiB: `derive` refused at 4,288, then 8,384, then
  11,456 MiB, each time "needs ~16 MiB more" — one read CHUNK's shortfall, printed as though it were the
  corpus's — and only 16,384 carried it. Two rules came out of it, both in `src/corpus/heapGuard.ts`:
  **extrapolate from what the read has already measured** (a partial read holds the best heap-per-disk-byte
  coefficient anyone will ever have for that corpus, and it self-corrects as the source mix changes), and
  **refuse on the band's floor but advise on its ceiling** — the preflight refuses at the floor so it can
  never turn away a corpus that would have fitted, which makes a suggestion built from that same floor the
  smallest ceiling that might work. Both refusal paths converge in one attempt on the real corpus
  (4,288 → 18,944 ✓; 6,656 → 12,800 ✓).

  **`bin/slopweaver` nevertheless sizes the ceiling from host RAM, as a LABELLED INTERIM**, under the same
  ruling, because the corpus outran the default within hours: V8's ceiling is a table lookup
  rather than a reading of the machine (4,288 MiB whether the host has 8 GiB or 48), so the launcher takes
  a quarter of physical memory, clamped to `[V8's default, 12 GiB]`, and an explicit flag always wins —
  the pair a cmux-launched session leaks into every child is not one, and `hooks/lib/cmux-node-options.sh`
  strips it first, or every seated `derive` runs at V8's default. The
  owner accepted it with an objection attached — a 12 GiB process is too much when three people can ask the
  bot a question at once on a 48 GiB laptop — so it ships with a **concurrency guard** (`src/lib/loadSlots.ts`): a ceiling one
  process may safely reach is not one three may reach, and the number of concurrent whole-corpus loads is
  capped at 75% of the machine divided by that ceiling. The cap is derived, never tuned — it rises on its
  own when a query holds less. Neither half is the fix; both keep the whole-corpus readers listed below
  working until each loads a slice.

  **A guard's PROJECTION must be bounded by something that is not the corpus.** Projecting a whole FILE
  from its size on disk at the band's ceiling uses `self`'s coefficient — text that is two-byte in V8 and
  carries no `raw`. Applied to a Slack window of about 2 GiB it demanded 3,716 MiB where the file actually retains
  about 1,073, and the reader refused a corpus that fits without reading a single Slack byte. A pessimistic
  coefficient is only cheap when the quantity it multiplies is small: the check runs between 8 MiB read chunks against the real isolate, so its
  projection is one chunk however big the file is, and no coefficient error can scale with the corpus.

- **A whole-in-memory `JSON.stringify` is a data-scale write in disguise.** `writeJsonFile` builds one
  string, copies it for the trailing newline, and copies it again into a `Buffer`. Measured on
  `silver/graph/graph.json` (a few hundred MiB, about a million edges): 3,982 MiB of RSS to write one file, which was the
  entire difference between a writing `derive` (5,701 MiB) and a `--dry-run` one (3,184 MiB), and the 1.1
  GiB swing between identical runs was collector timing over that transient. It was also **55% of the way
  into V8's 536,870,888-character maximum string length** — the same wall that stopped `compact` — on a
  corpus that only grows. That artefact had no reader anywhere in `src`, `docs` or `skills`, so the owner's
  ruling was to delete rather than stream it: a massive chunk of junk on disk that slows everything down, takes
  up space and provides no value. Without it
  `derive` runs at 3,458 MiB / 66.0 s. Its 3.6 MiB sibling `silver/graph/curated.json` went on a separate word
  of the owner's, since that ruling was about the massive dump; nothing writes `silver/graph/`. The general form of the class is the `whole-file-json` gate
  (`src/devLint/wholeFileJson.ts`).

- **A data-scale JSONL is STREAMED on the way out as well as on the way in.** Measured: a
  `slopweaver compact` that read each window file into one `utf8` string and composed the canonical file as
  one string printed `Cannot create a string longer than 0x1fffffe8 characters` against the live 5.9 GiB Slack
  window and left the source unfinished. A verb that only runs on corpora small enough not to need it is not
  a lifecycle. Compaction reads with `readJsonlLines` (one 8 MiB chunk plus one line, whatever the file
  weighs) and writes through `writeFileAtomicStream`, retaining one 32-hex dedup digest per survivor:
  measured through the real verb, about twenty Slack bronze windows holding about one and three-quarter million rows across roughly 7 GB became one
  canonical file of nearly the same row count in about a minute at under 1 GiB peak RSS, at V8's DEFAULT ceiling.

- **Measure post-GC live heap, never peak RSS, and measure a CURVE.** `slopweaver dev heap-curve` is the
  instrument: per-phase live heap against the corpus on disk, per source or whole, in either projection. Its
  exit code says whether the guard's coefficients still bracket what was measured; re-run it whenever the
  record shape, the reader or the index changes, and move the constants it names.
  `slopweaver dev dedup-index --source <source>` is its sibling on the WRITE side, and the pairing is the
  point: heap-curve measures what a query holds, dedup-index what a WRITE holds — the half that lets a bronze
  writer quietly outgrow its own corpus. Its exit code guards
  `DEDUP_INDEX_BYTES_PER_RECORD`, and it refuses to certify a build the heap guard cut short — a partial
  index divided by the rows it reached is a lower bound, and ratcheting a constant onto one would be the
  measurement lying in the safe-looking direction.
- **Price a slice off the corpus's own shape, never off its totals.** `slopweaver dev slice-shape` is the
  third instrument beside the two above, and it answers a different question: not what a stage HOLDS, but how
  much of the corpus a candidate dev-loop slice would keep. One streaming pass emits all four prices — a day
  histogram behind the `--days` windows, the per-source counts a `--limit` cap survives, the Slack
  per-thread size distribution, and the per-container tally an allowlist or a denylist would name. Measured
  on a couple of million records / about 9 GB: the whole pass is **about fifteen seconds at under 1 GB peak RSS**, so nothing about
  the corpus's SIZE is what makes the loop slow, and its `--days 7` price (tens of thousands of records) matched what
  `subset build --days 7` actually kept to within 2%. Two of its readings are the reason it exists rather
  than a totals table: the corpus is not recency-weighted (one month alone held over ten times the records
  of the month before it, so a month-granularity histogram would have priced every window wrong,
  which is why the buckets are DAYS), and one Slack container holds **nearly half of records and about two thirds of bytes**,
  so the totals in `by source` describe a distribution with a single dominant mode rather than a population.

- **A question holds almost nothing, because the database does the work** (the owner's retrieval ruling,
  [constraint 1a](hard.md#1a-retrieval-is-postgres-full-text-over-the-store-nothing-else)).
  A question sends one bounded SQL query to local Postgres and holds the page it gets back; there is no
  corpus load, no index, no encoder and no artefact on disk.

  **What still holds the whole corpus, and therefore still needs the raised ceiling:** `derive`, whose pipeline
  reads bronze through `readCorpusDir`. `distil`, `audit`, `slice verify`, `dev manifest` and the console's
  WORLD MODEL section stream the corpus census (`src/corpus/corpusCensus.ts`) — one skinny entry per logical
  record, each row read back by offset when it is needed — on the owner's ruling that bronze
  is sliced so nothing loads a multi-gigabyte corpus at once; `compact` streams line by line. Read the live
  list from the tree rather than from here: `rg -l 'readCorpusDir\(|loadCorpus\(' src --glob '!**/__tests__/**'`
  names every caller. `slopweaver dev heap-curve` measures what one of those passes actually retains, post-GC,
  against `HEAP_BYTES_PER_DISK_BYTE`, and exits non-zero when the shipped band no longer brackets it. The
  load slot (`src/cli/admitLoadSlot.ts`) still rations them, on the owner's ruling that slots are rationed and
  concurrent questions queue behind a load slot as a backstop — and on a 48 GiB laptop the derived
  limit is 3.

  The owner's ruling on the shape of all this: AI coding tends to bolt workarounds onto workarounds until a
  contraption stands where something simple and elegant would have done, and elegance in simplicity is what is
  wanted. A question's one `SELECT` is that shape.

## 8. Long-running work is observable

A verb that prints nothing is indistinguishable from a hung one. The owner could not tell 1% from 99% on a
ten-minute run, and neither could anyone else. Progress must be visible in the default invocation — not only on
the machine-readable lane, which is a real failure mode that has already happened.

Reuse the existing emitter (`src/lib/progressRichEmitter.ts` — three lanes, throttled, heartbeat + ETA). Do not build a
second one.

## 9. The house code standards hold

[AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) and the two rule files it links are not advisory: named-object params, no `any`,
explicit return types, zero mocks, no conditionals in assertions, exact-value assertions. The static-analysis
bar — every linter plus the bespoke ratchets, a 60-line function cap and cognitive complexity capped at 12 —
is whatever `yarn lint` runs today; read it there rather than trusting a count written here. A change that
diverges is a defect.
