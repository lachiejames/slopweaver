# 4 · Citations, proof and refusing to be confidently wrong

Part of [What the owner wants from the corpus — harvested from the owner's own voice](../corpus-intent.md).

## First-class, not decoration

A major first-class feature is that everything is citations cross-linking to each other: **everything verifiable,
everything falsifiable**.

The owner has no patience for anything unfalsifiable, and wants the system to embody **Newton's flaming laser
sword**: what cannot be settled by experimental observation is not worth debating.

Text alone as proof is not falsifiable, and proof that is unfalsifiable grades bronze or lower.

## Similarity nominates; it never concludes

Similarity can nominate, never conclude, and the owner agrees emphatically: **false positives kill trust
immediately**, so the architecture has to control for them. Never claim unknowingly; you cannot prove someone did
something in the absence of a message.

## Saying "I don't know" is a feature

Being able to say "I don't know" is very important. A human can say it, and a human who bluffs instead is
counterproductive, annoying and **trust-breaking**.

Knowing when to abstain rather than guess is hard: bots do not typically withhold an answer, they want to answer even
when unsure, and getting an AI to grade its own confidence is prone to problems.

The owner is against self-reported confidence percentages: an AI calculating its own percentage of confidence is
always misleading. The owner would break confidence into high, medium and perhaps low instead.

## Trust before autonomy

Trust grows before autonomy does. A system has to be trustworthy before it is automated, or it makes a mess, breaks
trust and ends up as another incumbent bot nobody uses.

The named failure mode is a bot its own users do not trust: an answer nobody trusts is wasted effort, however
often it is right.

## A citation should be something a human could have produced themselves

A screenshot serves as proof that the bot actually went and looked somewhere, and that anyone looking would reach the
same conclusion. Asked how many critical-severity vulnerabilities there are, the owner would go to exactly that link
and take exactly that screenshot. The goal is **letting the bot reach into GitHub and return an answer so well
grounded that nobody feels the need to fact-check it**.
