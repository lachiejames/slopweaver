# 6 · What "good" looks like for the two named-person questions

Part of [What the owner wants from the corpus — harvested from the owner's own voice](../corpus-intent.md).

## The demo bar the owner actually described

The owner pictures going to a colleague, saying the bot's new capabilities are ready to try, and picking a
question answerable from information somewhere in Notion, Slack or elsewhere. The bot should give an answer that
colleague judges high quality — **especially good if they already know what a high-quality answer looks like** —
and come back with a screenshot and citations to every source that backs it up.

A colleague is likeliest to want the bot improving their own work **if it can accurately answer questions with
citations**.

## For a leadership audience, the answer is the proposal's unlock

Nobody approves work like this until it demonstrates value, so the question is whether value can be shown with what
already exists. Asking the bot in Slack for a leader's top five pain points might produce a decent answer, and a
reader seeing it might recognise the same thing could be done for a customer.

## The reaction the owner is aiming for, on the harder class of question

Someone about to present at a retro asks the bot for three good examples of teams working on overlapping things and
losing time because they did not know. It comes back with three good examples, good enough to live-demo, and the room
reacts with **surprise at an example nobody knew about — an unknown unknown revealed.** That is the reaction the owner
wants.

The same goes for asking the bot to **tell you something you did not know about the org** and getting a genuinely
good answer rather than a poor one.

## And the honest floor

The owner accepts that omniscience is not the bar and that some misses are coverage, not ranking. People do not
always put information where it can be seen: some is in DMs, some in private channels without access. **The bot need
not be completely omniscient; it has to do the best with what it has.**
