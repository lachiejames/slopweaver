# Settings

The one place what Slopweaver connects to, the plans it thinks with, the console's theme, the orb's presence and a voice
call's behaviour are chosen. **Integrations** and **Models** read and write the machine through verbs (`secrets status
--all --json`, `slopweaver plans`); the three preferences are this browser's own stored choices, the same keys the page
and the call read, bar a `?stage=<presence>` preview address, which wins over the stored presence while it is in the
address.

Settings is a modal over whatever page you are on, opened from the sidebar's foot, the way claude.ai's settings sit over
its app, so a modal can sit on top of anything and you can stay on the main app with the sidebar and get a rough feel
for how these different things make the app look. Its sections run down the left in two groups — **Connect** (Integrations, Models) and **Preferences** (Theme, Presence,
The call) — under a "Search settings" box that keeps only the sections a query names, and the open one fills the right.
It opens on Integrations, the first tab. The scrim behind
it is light, so a theme or presence you press is seen landing on the page underneath. Escape, a click on the scrim or
the × closes it.

There is no Setup page and no first-run flow: those pages live in the settings modal as tabs, so everyone goes to
the right place without being forced through a sequence. While no plan is connected the sidebar's Settings entry carries a dot, and every empty page offers
**Connect a plan**, which opens Settings on Models; every page works without one. The old `/setup` address redirects to
`/queue#settings/models`.

The address carries it: `#settings/<section>` on any console page opens it there, and `#settings/integrations/<id>`
opens one integration's page, so a reload or a link reopens it where it was; a bare `#settings`, or a section it does
not draw, opens Integrations. Opening and closing change only that hash; the page under it keeps its context and any
card it holds open. A page with its own hash, such as `/workstreams#candidates`, has it carried inside the modal's
(`#settings/theme&page=candidates`) and gets it back when Settings closes, after a reload too.

<details>
<summary>Integrations</summary>

**Integrations** is a catalog of every integration Slopweaver reads, one card each with its status, behind a search box
and four filters — All, Connected, Needs attention and Available — each counting what it would show for the search in
hand. Needs attention sorts first, then A to Z (search yes, tags no). A card opens that integration's own
page over the catalog, Datadog's shape: a numbered **Configure** list — where to make the key, the field to paste it
into, the consent to run — beside what it is stored as, what reads it, what it can reach and how to disconnect it.

A status is read off which store files exist and the verb's verdict on each, plus whether Datadog's site is named
(the verb's `datadogSite`), never off a key being tried:

- **Connected** — everything it reads is stored and the store reports nothing wrong. Slack needs either token alone; its app-level token, which only lets the bot hear tags live, is optional, though a
  finding on it still needs attention.
  A consent door (Gmail, Drive, Calendar, Outlook) needs its grant and its client. Datadog needs its site too.
- **Needs attention** — some but not all of it is stored (one of Datadog's two keys), a consent grant sits without its
  client, Datadog's keys sit with no valid site, or the store reports a finding on any of its files (an expired grant
  cache, a loose file mode, a state this console does not know). Never counted under Connected, so a warning is never
  hidden among the healthy ones.
- **Available** — nothing of it is stored. A shared client alone (the Google one) starts none of its doors.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Models</summary>

Slopweaver runs on your own AI plans; it never buys one or bills you. Setting one up never touches a terminal.

**No plan connected.** Two cards, one per provider: what its plan does here, what it takes, and its **Connect**.
Codex's Connect starts `slopweaver plans connect --provider codex`, which asks Codex's own sign-in for its page and
opens it in your browser; signing in there is the consent. No model runs. Claude's card is drawn with its Connect
disabled and says signing in from here comes next.

**One or more connected.** Each plan's row with its usage-limit drop-down, 10% to 100% in tens, pre-selected at the
limit the plan already carries, so a personal plan can take a hundred percent and an enterprise plan fifty. A choice is written at once with `plans set-usage-limit`, passing `--from` the limit it was drawn
at, so a choice made off an older reading is refused rather than written over a newer one; one choice is written at a
time. A Codex login found on this Mac is never a plan: a plan comes only from your own sign-in.
On an install whose plans were found on this Mac but never saved, the drop-downs are locked and **Keep these plans**
writes them down (`plans adopt`). Below the rows, **+ Codex plan** connects another.

**A sign-in in flight.** One row follows the attempt: its step while it waits on you, **Open the sign-in page again**
if you closed it, **Cancel**, and its end in plain words — connected as which account, or why it failed, with what
Codex said and **Try again**. It polls every 2 seconds only while an attempt is in flight, and re-reads the plan list
once when one connects.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Theme, Presence and The call</summary>

Nine colours, light and dark and a glassiness percentage are confusing and maybe the wrong options, and every
combination of them can never be made to look right. So **Theme** offers a handful of
whole appearances, each one picked so its parts look right together, and one press wears the whole of it — no apply
button, no undo.

A **Presence** tab dictates what happens inside the orb; the presence and the theme are decoupled, so the presence
choice sticks and the theme supplies its colours; and there are six options for theme and six for presence. So there are six of each, chosen apart, and
any presence under any theme holds together: every presence is drawn as grey light and the theme's two orb colours
alone colour it.

No theme carries a glassiness: every console surface is opaque,
and the glass is its edges and shadow alone.

**Theme.** Six: Night sky, Deep sea, Ember, Dusk, Daylight and Graphite. Each tile previews its theme two ways at
once, both filled with the presence you wear now: the orb full screen on that theme's own ground, and in its bottom
right the corner orb on the dark disc it sits on over every other app. A tile is ringed in its accent when it is the
theme you wear now; when you wear none of them — stored choices that match no theme — a line says so. A first visit
wears **Night sky**, the blue-tinted dark, the owner's pairing. Graphite is the sixth.

**Presence.** Six: Star cluster, Mist, Ink, Dot matrix, Aurora and Embers. Each tile is the orb filled with that
presence, live, on the page's own ground and lit in the theme you wear now; one press wears it on the stage and in the
corner at once, and it stays through every theme change. A first call wears the **star cluster**. Embers is the sixth; a stored presence that names none of the six opens on the star cluster.

**The call** section: a switch for each thing the full-screen stage shows and the orb's badge sounds. The glow round
the screen's edge has no switch, since it is a constant. The stage has no settings of its own and only honours these (a second place for preferences clutters the call and may conflict with what is being said
on it). Every change reaches a call already up. Where the voice opens is no setting: it stays in whichever state you
left it — the orb, the middle or full screen ([the call widget](/docs/console/call-widget)).

A theme's four choices are written one after another in a single press, not as one transaction. Every open window
re-reads storage on each change it hears and settles on the whole theme; only a press interrupted between two writes
can leave a mix, which Theme then shows as worn by none. A presence is one choice under its own key.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What every field is derived from</summary>

| In the modal      | Derived from                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ----------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Open, and where   | the window's hash, `#settings/<section>[/<item>]`, read by `settingsSectionOf` and `settingsItemOf` (`site/lib/settingsModal.ts`); any other hash is closed                                                          |
| A section search  | each section's label and keywords, every word of the query in them (`settingsSectionsMatching`)                                                                                                                      |
| An integration    | one row of `INTEGRATIONS` (`site/lib/integrations.ts`) and its guide in `INTEGRATION_GUIDES` (`site/lib/integrationGuides.ts`)                                                                                       |
| Its status        | the `secrets` live read (`secrets status --all --json`), each file's `stored` and `state`, judged by `integrationStatusOf` (`site/lib/integrationCatalog.ts`)                                                        |
| Plans             | `slopweaver plans list --json`, read by `readPlanLimits` (`site/components/live/readPlanLimits.ts`)                                                                                                                  |
| A sign-in         | `slopweaver plans connections --json`, read by `readPlanConnections`; its words are `connectionLineOf` (`site/lib/planConnect.ts`)                                                                                   |
| Plans acts        | `plans-connect-codex`, `plans-connect-cancel`, `plans-adopt` and `plans-set-usage-limit` (`site/lib/actVerbs.ts`)                                                                                                    |
| The Settings dot  | `noPlanConnectedOf` (`site/lib/pageEmpty.ts`) over the plans reading, polled on the sidebar's cadence                                                                                                                |
| A theme           | one row of `THEME_PRESETS` (`site/lib/themePresets.ts`): a colour family, light or dark, the orb's two colours, a corner backing and its strength                                                                    |
| A tile's ground   | that family and mode's palette in `THEME_PALETTES` (`site/lib/themePalettes.ts`), drawn as the stage draws its ground                                                                                                |
| A tile's orbs     | the presence worn now (`site/lib/orbPresence.ts`), lit in that theme's two colours set on the tile itself (`orbRampStyle`, `site/lib/orbRamp.ts`), resting, at the stage's and the corner's own calm                 |
| The orb's colours | two inherited custom properties, `--sw-orb-ramp-dark` and `--sw-orb-ramp-light`, which the appearance stylesheet sets per family and mode (`appearanceStylesheet`, `site/lib/theme.ts`) and the orb reads each frame |
| Theme worn now    | every one of the four stored choices equals that theme's (`presetWorn`)                                                                                                                                              |
| A theme press     | writes each of the four under its own key — `sw-theme-family`, `sw-theme-mode` and the two `slopweaver.talk.orbBacking*` keys — so the voice's window and every other open window take it at once                    |
| A presence        | one entry of `ORB_PRESENCES` (`site/lib/orbPresence.ts`), drawn by its painter (`site/components/voice/presencePainters.ts`)                                                                                         |
| Presence worn now | the stored `slopweaver.talk.presence.v1`, else the star cluster; a valid `?stage=<presence>` preview address wins over it (`presenceWorn`)                                                                           |
| The call          | the stage's own stored switches — each of `STAGE_TOGGLES` (`site/lib/voiceStage.ts`) — and the badge's sound (`components/voice/OrbCues.tsx`), the very keys the stage reads                                         |

</details>
