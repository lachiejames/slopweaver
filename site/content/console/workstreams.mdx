# Workstreams

Your outcomes as one list of collapsed cards, at risk first: whose move each one is, how many of its own milestones are done, and the dated story behind it on click. Read from `slopweaver workstreams list --json`, out of the store.

<details>
<summary>What this page shows</summary>

A page to remember every workstream you drive, and one that shows actual things progressed to
actual outcomes. So each card opens to the
**outcome** other people would agree improves the business, names who already agrees, and carries the whole life
of the work, including the part after the code ships.

**One vertical list, like the Queue.** Each collapsed card carries, on its left, a coloured chip
naming whose move it is: **Your call** (held on a decision only you can make), **Chase** (held on someone named),
**Stalled** (stalled with nobody named), then **Discovery**, **Moving**, **Done**, **Kept here only** and
**Abandoned**. Beside it the title, a thin meter under it filled to the share of its own milestones done, and a few
stats: its progress line (`1 of 3 done, next: First batch merged`), when it last moved, who it waits on, its
priority, initiative and tags. The list is ordered at risk first (your call, chase, stalled), then by your priority
within each, then the register's order. The context switcher filters it: a `team` workstream never shows under
Slopweaver.

**Every workstream has its own milestones, and no generic bar**. The milestone `done` is
its definition of done, and every other one leads to it. A milestone is done once a dated entry finishing it links
its proof. Under a card's meter, a strip carries one pill per milestone, the ones that can run side by side stacked
together and an arrow to the ones that wait on them (milestones run in parallel rather than in a line, some still in order). Its slide-over draws the graph: a box per milestone saying where it is (done on a
day, moving since a day, ready to start, or what it waits on) and an arrow along each dependency, solid once the
one it leaves is done. A story entry that moves one names it, and one that finishes it says so.

**A workstream can end without its outcome**, and "done" is the wrong word for it. An abandoned card is the quietest on the page: a dashed grey chip, its title greyed with your reason and
the day under it, its meter faded, and it sits last. It is never counted under **Done**; it has its own number. Its
slide-over opens on an **Ended:** line with the reason and where you said it, then the story it had.

Above the list, the hero numbers count what the context shows: **At risk** first, then each lane holding
anything. Each is a button that filters to what it counts and names those workstreams and the rule on hover.
Under them, dropdowns for state, priority, initiative and tag, all shown by default, each in the address. Every
dated entry's source is a citation chip with its hover card. The register's grouping, when it names one, is a line
at the foot of the page.

Clicking a card opens its slide-over, exactly as a Queue card does, addressed by `?open=<id>` with every filter
kept: the outcome, its progress against its own milestones, where it is and what it waits on above the fold, then
its dated story, its feedback — the negative beside the positive, with what you did about it — and a skeptic's line,
with its priority, initiative, its **stakeholders** as identity chips with the part each plays (hover one for their
card), the **related** workstreams, each opening its own slide-over with why it relates, and its **links**: each a
chip naming what it points at, a Queue or Alignment card, an automation, a pull request or a Linear project.
A finished or handed-over one stands level with a live one (a completion is something you keep and
filter for, never tucked away): its card carries a **closed** pill with the day, and its slide-over its
**Outcome**, the day it **Closed**, where it went, whether it still holds, its cited entries, stakeholders and
related workstreams, each said as not recorded where it was never kept. No card carries a slug or worktree id line,
and no citation shows a Slack channel id: a Slack source reads as the channel you know, `a message in
#proj-monitoring`.

**Hovering a Slack source opens a window into that channel as it is now** (what Slack desktop would
show, images included, so you never leave localhost). The card reads the newest page live through `slack
channel-view`, oldest at the top and scrolled to the newest, each author named and each picture drawn from Slack's
own preview, which opens larger on a click. A file it does not draw says why (a PDF, deleted, no access, past
the sixteen-picture limit), the head says when it was read and opens the channel in Slack, and a read that fails
says so rather than showing an empty channel.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What every figure is derived from</summary>

| On the page                | Derived from                                                                                                                                          |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A meter's fill             | the share of the workstream's own milestones done (`derived.progress`); a milestone is done once an entry finishing it (`done`) links its proof       |
| The progress line          | `derived.progressLine`: how many of its own milestones are done, and the next one ready to start                                                      |
| Started · last moved       | the earliest and latest dated entry                                                                                                                   |
| The lane chip              | `waitingOn` (you → your call, someone named → chase), else a stalled or discovery status, else moving; local and closing kinds by kind                |
| An abandoned card          | the workstream's `ending`, recorded by `workstreams react "<n> abandoned" --reason --anchor`; it outranks any hold, so an ended card is nobody's move |
| A closing card's day       | its `closedOn`, the day `react "<n> done"` placed it; one never recorded says not recorded                                                            |
| The order                  | at-risk lanes first, then your stated priority (urgent, high, normal, parked; one not stated sits after normal), then the register's order            |
| A hero number              | the workstreams the context shows in that lane, or in every at-risk lane for **At risk**                                                              |
| Which context shows a card | the `workspace` each workstream states, team or self                                                                                                  |
| A stakeholder chip         | the Slack member id the workstream names in `people`, joined to the member roster; a member it does not hold is said as such, never by id             |
| A related workstream       | a `workstream` link, its label the reason it relates                                                                                                  |
| A link chip                | every other link: its kind and its canonical target (a card's slug, an automation's recipe id, `owner/repo#N`, a Linear project's id)                 |
| A Slack citation's words   | the register document's `channels`, each conversation's name by id; the verb refuses a Slack link into a conversation that map does not name          |
| A linked gauge             | the workstream's own reading of somebody else's measure (value, of, gate, the source and when it was read), and how far short of the gate it is       |
| An own milestone's state   | the entries naming it: done once one finishes it with its link, moving once any names it, else ready or waiting on its `after`                        |
| An own milestone's column  | the longest chain of `after` before it, so milestones that can run side by side share a column; the register refuses a cycle                          |
| Status, outcome, skeptic   | written into the register from a reading of the evidence; the four status words are proven, validating, stalled, discovery                            |

Every figure is computed by the verb (`src/workstreams/store/projection.ts`), each lane and the order by
`src/workstreams/lanes.ts`; the page only draws. Nothing on the page is a typed percentage, so a meter cannot drift
from its sources, and every dated entry is one click from the message, pull request or ticket it cites. The page
reads schema version 2 alone: a document of any other version, or one missing a field it should carry, draws a
failed read rather than a card with a blank where a fact was.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Proposed workstreams: the outcomes you stated and what a mine of your history found, to keep or drop</summary>

Above the tiles, at `/workstreams#candidates`, a collapsed **Proposed workstreams** section holds every proposal,
read from `slopweaver workstreams candidates --json`: first the outcomes you have stated out loud, each in your own
words and anchored to the turn you said it, then every workstream a session mined from the person's connected
history. They are the ONE way an outcome reaches the
register — nothing is written until you keep one; keep or drop each when you next open Workstreams. Its one line
counts the candidates, the unanswered, each answer given (shown only when nonzero) and what the seat still owes;
under it, the question — accept as a workstream, with these definitions of done — and where the proposals came from:
the outcomes you stated, then, once a mine has run, which sources it read, with how many records each.

It opens on the **three** unanswered candidates with the most of the person's own words behind them, the rest behind
one link as one ranked list ("seventy workstreams?! I ain't reading all that"), or one at a time with previous and
next. A card shows its number, title, period, evidence count and, open, the outcome, the dated quotes in their own
words, their earlier steers with dates, what looks stalled, the owning team the miner guessed and who the first
conversation is with.

Its definition-of-done options each show their lens (the result, the people across it and owning it, or adoption),
trade-off and stretch rung; **all of them** asks which comes first; one recorded in their own words shows read-only,
and _None of these? Say what done means in the call's chat._ The six words — accept, not mine, sort of, not sure,
let's talk, already done — press the chosen definition first, then the word. Below them: place it as a milestone,
outcome or same-as of a register workstream (the register listed beside it); decline as covered by one; accept as
answering an earlier steer; and draft each owed conversation onto the Queue, which sends nothing. Every one is a
click or a pick from a list. An answer that needs words — a new title, whose it is, superseded by what, who first, a
proposal of your own (_To propose another, say it in the call's chat._) — has no box: the console's one text input
is the call's chat , and a touched or opened card is the page's open item, `candidate:<id>`, so
what you say there names it and the supervisor runs the verb. Every press runs one `workstream-*` row through the act list, so
a press and a relayed word write the same ledgers; a yes or done re-reads the tiles. Every candidate already ruled
sits behind ONE collapsed **Already ruled** section, never mixed in with the unruled: each line its ruling in one clause, the date and your quoted words, opening to its card to change it. The
ruling is the reactions ledger's latest row, the one field both the page and the voice read. The person's own
proposals sit in their own fold. Before any mine has run the section holds the stated outcomes alone, and says so.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Where the register lives, and why it is not the board</summary>

In the store, one row per workstream with its own milestones, entries and links, and one register document (`meta`)
naming its channels, grouping and initiatives. `slopweaver workstreams put` writes it in one transaction, as does the owner's
yes on a proposed candidate (`slopweaver workstreams react`), both validating every rule first and writing nothing
when one fails. It lives under the home rather than in git because every line is an internal quote or link.

The handover board is the only register of work in flight (`docs/workstreams.md`); this holds outcomes, which outlive every board row under them, and it is the ONE source of them: the supervisor's coverage reads the outcomes kept here, each with the board rows its membership names. The fleet's own placement against the board is on
[Fleet](/docs/console/fleet), and a board row's citation reads as the row's words, opening no page.

A store holding no workstream yet is an answer, not a failure (`NO_WORKSTREAMS`, exit 3), and so is a register whose
document lists none: the page draws the console's designed empty state, saying a workstream appears when you keep
one of the proposals above, the outcomes you have stated among them, and that nothing is kept until you do — with
**Connect a plan**, which opens Settings on Models, while no plan is connected, and never a command to run. A store
that refuses draws a staleness mark, never an empty page. A register whose document is not written yet draws its
workstreams with no channel names, grouping or initiatives, which is what the store holds.

</details>
