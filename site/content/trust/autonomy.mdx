# Graduated autonomy

On the owner's own pull requests the fleet clears CodeRabbit changes, PR comments, red CI, conflicts, rebases and
restacks **itself**, and merges or enqueues a mergeable one unasked. A red or behind pull request with a
review ask already out is fixed, never carded — a card there holds things up on the owner.

**Reeled in**: a Slack message to a person that nobody asked for — a review ask, a chase, a
bump, a DM as the owner — and a Linear closure that is arguable are **parked on the owner's queue** for their yes
(`slopweaver slack draft record`), until the owner approves the pattern. Any other obvious team-facing act needs no say-so.

## The criterion

Obvious to a session that knows the owner → **do it**, recorded. Unclear → **card it**.

**Whose idea it was is the line.** The owner's steer self-merges. A bug the fleet went and found lands with
a ledger row and no card. A finding aimed at a colleague still needs the owner's own word — and a chair relay never
is that word.

## Recording is the half that makes it work

```bash
slopweaver autonomy record    # never a shell redirect
slopweaver autonomy list      # what the fleet has done without asking
```

The ledger is the register the console's [Alignment page](/docs/console/alignment) draws its act cards from. An
act that was right and unrecorded looks identical to one that never happened.

The owner's retroactive word on an act is given on that page, which blocks nothing, and never as a card on their Queue.

## What still does not widen

No tier-3 rail widens either way. New or widened access, a changed purpose, egress to a new audience,
unauthorised spend, an irreversible unasked-for act, and anything a rule reserves for the owner — those stay the owner's,
and a ledger row is never authority for one.

## An ask that changes the direction the owner agreed

That is not autonomy at all, it is the **phase rule**: publish a page, take the owner's reaction, then act. The
difference is whether you are executing the direction or changing it.

## Journalled grants — a decision the owner has already made

When the owner rules in another session, that session writes it down as a row rather than relaying the words.

```bash
slopweaver grant show <id>       # what you read BEFORE you act
slopweaver grant perform <id> --wrote "<exactly what you wrote>"
slopweaver grant refuse <id> --check <CODE> --why "<what you observed>"
```

A row buys **origin, not authority**. `perform` compares your read-back to the row byte for byte and
journals nothing on a mismatch. A refusal names **which check** failed, from a closed set — and there is
deliberately no code for disbelieving the sender, so "I don't believe you" is not an available shape.

**Never ask the owner to type an instruction into your own tab.** The owner only speaks to the supervisor, and a session
that holds work until the owner context-switches into a third window has cost them the exact thing this mechanism
removes.
