# The autonomy ledger

[Graduated autonomy](/docs/trust/autonomy) is the rule about what the fleet may do unasked. This is the register
that makes it steerable: every act taken under it, newest first, one line each, and every one the owner has not yet
given a word on drawn as a card on the console's [Alignment page](/docs/console/alignment).

<AutonomyLedger />

## What it is for, which is not what a log is for

It exists so the owner can see what the fleet did under graduated autonomy and give a retroactive steer: on
something the owner would have been fine with that the fleet did not do, or on something it did do that the
owner was not fine with.

Three consequences, each a rule rather than a note:

- **It lives in the console, beside the owner's Queue**, as the Alignment page's act cards. A ledger on its own
  artifact is one the owner would look at only on the day it was built.
- **It is not folded away.** On the fleet briefing every other section below the cards is behind a fold and
  this one is not, because a steering surface the owner never meets collects nothing.
- **It is simpler than a card.** One line, the object acted on, an instant, a session. No question, no
  options, no lean — there is nothing to answer, only something to disagree with.

## Recording, and then steering

```bash
slopweaver autonomy record            # as you act — never a shell redirect
slopweaver autonomy list --unsteered  # what the fleet has done that the owner has not reacted to
slopweaver autonomy steer <id> --steer "<the owner's words>" --by-session <you>
```

`list --unsteered` is the sweep's own question. **It is not a queue to drain and it never becomes a card** —
the owner steers when they happen to read it, and the register remembers until they do.

The owner does not run commands off the page. The owner tells you, and you record their words **as close to
verbatim as you can**: a paraphrase is where a preference quietly becomes something else. The row then says the
owner has steered it, and carries that word in its fold.

The owner also gives a word on an act in the box on its card on the Alignment page, which runs the same
`autonomy steer` for them. Acts are never on the Queue, which is for actions for the owner.

## What a row shows, and what it holds back

| Above the line                                      | Behind the fold                                                     |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| what was done, on which object, when, by which seat | the said, then the why, the witness, the full reference, the act id |

**The fold opens on the said, in the owner's words, and the why sits beneath it.** They are two accounts of one
act written for two readers — the said for the owner, the why for the next session — and showing the second where
the first belongs takes this section off the page for the owner. The said is inside the fold
rather than on the visible line because the visible line is what the word budget counts, not because it
ranks below the why. A row recorded before the register asked for a said renders none, and says so.

**Only a URL subject is a link.** A subject is a checkable id in one of three forms — an absolute URL, a
board row slug, or a path under the state home — and the other two render as code rather than as an anchor,
because a link that scrolls nowhere teaches the owner to distrust every other link on the page. The full reference
is in the fold either way.

The three visible labels are **bounded**; the why, the witness and the owner's own words are not, because they sit
in a fold nothing forces the owner through. The said is the one folded field that IS bounded, and not by the page:
the register refuses a longer line at the door. If the page runs over its word budget, shorten the lede or fold a
recent block — never the ledger.

## A refused act is a real row

An act the fleet tried and could not complete renders as exactly that, and the renderer never presents one
as a success. Do not tidy those out of the owner's view: they are among the things the owner must be able to steer.

## When the whole section disappears

The reader **refuses a malformed line rather than skipping it**, so one bad row takes the section off the
page and the render says why, naming the line. That is the design — a page reporting "nothing was done under
autonomy" because a read failed is the confident wrong answer this section exists to prevent.

The repair reports what it would change and writes nothing until you ask it to apply. It is the same verb
that adopted the fifty-three hand-written acts standing when the register landed.

**Omitting the ledger flag renders no section at all**, which is a real state and not the same as an empty
register. The check cannot tell the two apart from the rendered bytes — so if you meant to show it, look for
the heading rather than trusting a clean run.

## One way it makes a card stop resolving

A bare pull-request reference in a hand-written card resolves only while the whole page names exactly one
repository. An act on a repository no card mentions adds a second one, and the gate then correctly refuses
the shorthand. That is the gate working: with two repositories on the page, a bare number genuinely is
ambiguous. Write the card's reference in full — never drop the ledger row to make a card's shorthand
survive.
